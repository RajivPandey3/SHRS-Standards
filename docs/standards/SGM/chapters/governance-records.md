# Chapter 6 — Governance Evidence, Records & Traceability

## 6.1 Purpose

This chapter establishes the constitutional governance framework for governance evidence, governance records, and traceability within the Standardized Hierarchical Repository System (SHRS).

It defines the evidence required to demonstrate that governance activities have been performed in accordance with this standard.

Governance evidence SHALL provide the constitutional basis for verification, review, audit, publication integrity, and long-term preservation.

---

## 6.2 Governance Evidence Model

Governance evidence SHALL constitute the authoritative proof that governance activities have been properly performed.

Governance evidence SHALL include:

- governance records;
- work orders;
- change requests;
- review records;
- approval records;
- publication records;
- release records; and
- traceability relationships.

Governance evidence SHALL remain complete, consistent, and permanently traceable.

---

## 6.3 Governance Records

Every governance activity SHALL produce one or more governance records.

Governance records SHALL:

- identify the governance activity;
- identify the responsible governance role;
- identify the applicable lifecycle state;
- identify the applicable standard;
- preserve governance decisions; and
- remain version controlled.

Governance records SHALL constitute the official governance history.

---

## 6.4 Record Classification

Governance records SHALL be classified according to their governance function.

Record classes MAY include:

- Work Order Records;
- Change Request Records;
- Review Records;
- Approval Records;
- Publication Records;
- Release Records;
- Governance Decision Records; and
- Audit Records.

Additional record classes MAY be established through approved governance decisions.

---

## 6.5 Traceability Model

Every governance record SHALL participate in the governance traceability model.

Traceability SHALL enable verification of relationships between:

- governance decisions;
- work orders;
- change requests;
- reviews;
- approvals;
- publications;
- releases; and
- published editions.

No mandatory governance activity SHALL exist without traceable governance evidence.

---

## 6.6 Evidence Integrity

Governance evidence SHALL preserve:

- authenticity;
- completeness;
- consistency;
- immutability of approved records;
- version integrity; and
- long-term reliability.

Governance evidence SHALL be protected against unauthorized modification.

---

## 6.7 Canonical Repository Evidence

The canonical repository SHALL constitute the authoritative governance evidence repository.

The canonical repository SHALL preserve:

- governance records;
- lifecycle records;
- publication history;
- revision history;
- release history; and
- governance metadata.

Generated publication artifacts SHALL NOT replace the canonical governance evidence.

---

## 6.8 Record Retention

Governance evidence SHALL be retained for the duration required to preserve governance traceability.

Retention policies SHALL ensure that historical governance decisions remain independently verifiable.

Governance evidence SHALL NOT be discarded where doing so would compromise constitutional traceability.

---

## 6.9 Archival

Superseded editions, withdrawn standards, and historical governance records SHALL remain archived.

Archived governance evidence SHALL remain:

- identifiable;
- retrievable;
- traceable; and
- protected against unauthorized modification.

Archival SHALL preserve historical governance integrity.

---

## 6.10 Auditability

Governance evidence SHALL support independent governance audit.

Governance audits SHALL be capable of verifying:

- governance authority;
- lifecycle compliance;
- publication integrity;
- traceability;
- record completeness; and
- constitutional conformance.

Audit conclusions SHALL themselves become governance records.

---

## 6.11 Compliance

Organizations implementing SHRS governance SHALL establish governance evidence systems consistent with this chapter.

Failure to preserve mandatory governance evidence or traceability SHALL constitute non-conformance.

---

**End of Chapter 6**
