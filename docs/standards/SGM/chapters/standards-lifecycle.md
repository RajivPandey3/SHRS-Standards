# Chapter 4 — Standards Lifecycle & Change Governance

## 4.1 Purpose

This chapter establishes the normative governance framework governing the lifecycle of SHRS standards.

It defines the lifecycle states, governance transitions, change control mechanisms, review governance, approval governance, version governance, and lifecycle records applicable to every normative standard developed under SHRS.

The lifecycle defined by this chapter SHALL apply to all normative standards unless explicitly exempted by an approved governance decision.

---

## 4.2 Lifecycle Principles

The lifecycle of every SHRS standard SHALL be governed by the following operational principles:

- controlled progression;
- documented governance actions;
- complete traceability;
- independent review;
- reproducible publication;
- constitutional consistency; and
- long-term maintainability.

No lifecycle transition SHALL occur without satisfying the governance requirements applicable to the current lifecycle state.

---

## 4.3 Lifecycle States

Every SHRS standard SHALL exist in exactly one lifecycle state at any point in time.

The lifecycle states defined by this standard are:

1. Proposed
2. Draft
3. Review
4. Approved
5. Published
6. Maintained
7. Deprecated
8. Withdrawn

Each lifecycle state SHALL have defined entry criteria, governance responsibilities, and exit conditions.

---

## 4.4 Lifecycle State Transitions

Lifecycle states SHALL transition only through approved governance actions.

The permitted lifecycle progression is:

Proposed → Draft → Review → Approved → Published → Maintained

A maintained standard MAY subsequently transition to:

- Revised;
- Deprecated; or
- Withdrawn.

Direct transitions bypassing mandatory lifecycle states SHALL NOT be permitted unless expressly authorized through an Exceptional Governance Action defined by this standard.

---

## 4.5 Governance Gates

Every lifecycle transition SHALL pass through one or more governance gates.

Governance gates SHALL verify that all mandatory governance requirements for the current lifecycle stage have been satisfied.

The minimum governance gates are:

- Editorial Completion Gate;
- Technical Review Gate;
- Approval Gate; and
- Publication Gate.

Failure to satisfy a governance gate SHALL prevent progression to the subsequent lifecycle state.

---

## 4.6 Work Orders

All normative engineering activities SHALL be performed under an approved Work Order.

A Work Order SHALL define:

- Work Order Identifier;
- Objective;
- Scope;
- Deliverables;
- Responsible Governance Role;
- Acceptance Criteria;
- Status; and
- Closure Record.

No normative modification SHALL be introduced without an associated Work Order.

---

## 4.7 Change Requests

Every proposed normative modification SHALL originate from a documented Change Request.

A Change Request SHALL include:

- Change Request Identifier;
- Description of the proposed change;
- Rationale;
- Impact assessment;
- Affected standards;
- Proposed disposition; and
- Decision record.

Accepted Change Requests SHALL be implemented only through an approved Work Order.

---

## 4.8 Review Governance

Every normative modification SHALL undergo review before approval.

The governance framework recognizes the following review classes:

- Editorial Review;
- Technical Review; and
- Constitutional Review, where constitutional provisions are affected.

Review outcomes SHALL be documented within the governance record.

---

## 4.9 Approval Governance

Approval SHALL occur only after successful completion of all mandatory governance reviews.

Approval decisions SHALL identify:

- approving authority;
- approval date;
- approved version;
- supporting governance records; and
- applicable lifecycle state.

An approval SHALL authorize progression only to the next permitted lifecycle state.

---

## 4.10 Version Governance

Every lifecycle transition resulting in a normative change SHALL produce an updated version designation.

Version identifiers SHALL remain unique, traceable, and permanently associated with the approved governance record.

Edition changes SHALL represent new approved baselines.

Minor revisions, amendments, and corrigenda SHALL preserve traceability to the originating edition.

---

## 4.11 Deprecation and Withdrawal

A published standard MAY be deprecated where continued use is no longer recommended.

A deprecated standard MAY subsequently be withdrawn.

Withdrawal SHALL preserve historical governance records and publication archives.

Withdrawal SHALL NOT remove governance traceability.

---

## 4.12 Exceptional Governance Actions

Exceptional Governance Actions MAY be authorized only where ordinary lifecycle progression cannot reasonably address urgent governance requirements.

Exceptional actions SHALL:

- identify the exceptional circumstances;
- define the approving authority;
- preserve governance traceability; and
- remain fully documented.

Exceptional Governance Actions SHALL NOT permanently bypass constitutional governance requirements.

---

## 4.13 Lifecycle Records

Every lifecycle transition SHALL generate one or more governance records.

Lifecycle records SHALL remain:

- complete;
- version controlled;
- auditable;
- permanently traceable; and
- preserved within the canonical repository.

The lifecycle records defined by this chapter SHALL constitute the operational evidence of governance.

---

## 4.14 Compliance

Organizations implementing SHRS governance SHALL ensure that every normative standard follows the lifecycle defined by this chapter.

Any deviation from the prescribed lifecycle SHALL require documented governance approval and SHALL remain permanently traceable.

Failure to comply with mandatory lifecycle requirements SHALL constitute non-conformance.

---

**End of Chapter 4**
